Technical expertise, social responsibility.

Engineers Without Borders at Cornell is a student-led chapter of EWB-USA, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. We build sustainable engineering solutions in direct partnership with communities.

01 / Mission

Engineering skills are most valuable when they reach the people with the least access to them.

We combine technical expertise with a strong sense of social responsibility, working directly with communities to address critical infrastructure challenges and promote long-term resilience.

As an active chapter of EWB-USA, we uphold the highest standards of ethical engineering, sustainability, and community partnership. Every project is built on cooperation, transparency, and mutual respect, and our members work every stage of the lifecycle: needs assessment, technical design, implementation, and evaluation.

02 / Who We Are

A community united by service.

From focused work sessions to relaxed socials on Libe Slope, our engagements have built a tenacious connection, not only with the communities we serve, but with each other.

We come from a variety of states, social backgrounds, colleges, and fields of study, united under a shared commitment to service.

03 / What We Stand For

Principles that guide every project.

Community-driven

Communities define the need and co-own the solution. We require a local contribution and a maintenance partner before we build.

Built to last

Every design is judged by one question: will it still serve people years later? Durability and maintainability come first.

Students lead

Members run the engineering, fundraising, and logistics, gaining real leadership and project-management experience.

Ethical practice

As an EWB-USA chapter, we hold to rigorous standards of safety, transparency, and responsible engineering.

04 / Over The Years

Sixteen years of building with communities.

2009

The Cornell chapter is founded

Students start the Cornell University chapter of Engineers Without Borders.

2011 · Calcha, Bolivia

International origins

Built a walking bridge to improve the safety and productivity of agricultural fields, and optimized water management for cleaner, more reliable fresh water.

2014 · Binghamton, NY

Going domestic: EPOD

Partnered with Every1sHome to design an inexpensive, portable shelter for the local homeless population.

2016 · Erie, PA

Aquaponics challenge

Designed an aquaponics greenhouse facility and affordable, sustainable refrigeration equipment.

2017 · Sunuka, Tanzania

Solar irrigation

Implemented an irrigation system to address food and economic insecurity for a farming community.

2019 · Sub-Saharan Africa

Digital agriculture begins

Launched a machine-learning drone and rover system to detect Northern Leaf Blight in maize before it spreads.

2020 · Pine Ridge, SD

Sustainable housing

Partnered with First Families Now to design a cost-effective, permanent Sustainable Housing Unit.

2021 to 2024 · Finger Lakes

Software for sustainability

Built carbon-offset tools with Sustainable Tompkins and the Tompkins Climate Fund, the Cornell Wardrobe app, and a Finger Lakes ReUse donation app.

2025 · Barrio San Cristóbal

Sanitation initiative

Visited San Cristóbal and adopted a multi-year sanitation project, now entering its implementation phase.
