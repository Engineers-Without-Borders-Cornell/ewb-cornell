Pine Ridge
Sustainable Housing

Designed and delivered a Sustainable Housing Unit (SHU) for the Pine Ridge Reservation in South Dakota, a four-person home built with affordable, durable methods and Lakota design input, in response to overcrowding on the reservation.

Project Overview

Overcrowding and substandard housing affect thousands of Oglala Lakota Sioux tribe members on the Pine Ridge Reservation, and many families live in homes that are structurally unsafe and expensive to heat. Any design has to answer both the engineering problem and what the community wants a home to be.

EWB Cornell's Domestic Subteam partnered with EWB-USA and tribal community members to design a Sustainable Housing Unit (SHU) that is affordable to build and maintain, cheap to heat, and shaped by tribal input. The completed unit is a working home and a template the partner can build again.

The Challenge

  • Severe housing shortage leads to overcrowding with multiple families sharing single units
  • Existing housing stock is often structurally inadequate and energy-inefficient
  • Extreme weather in South Dakota demands heavy insulation and reliable heating
  • Limited local construction materials and supply chains constrain design options
  • The design has to reflect Lakota cultural identity and tribal self-determination

The Sustainable Housing Unit

The SHU is a four-person home with a kitchen, bathroom, and living space laid out to get the most usable area from a small footprint. Material and construction choices keep build cost and long-term maintenance down.

Architectural drawings and engineering documentation

Key Design Features

  • High-performance insulation for South Dakota's extreme temperature range (−20°F to 100°F)
  • Passive solar design principles to reduce heating energy consumption
  • Durable, low-maintenance exterior materials suited to the local climate
  • Kitchen, bathroom, and living spaces in a functional four-person layout
  • Full construction documentation so the partner can build more
  • Design developed in partnership with tribal leaders and residents

Community Partnership

Throughout the project, the Domestic Subteam worked in close partnership with EWB-USA and reservation community members to ensure the design reflected Lakota priorities and values. Community sessions drove layout decisions and material choices, so the SHU was designed by the people who would live in it.

Results

The completed SHU provides safe, sustainable housing for a family that had previously been living in overcrowded conditions. The team also produced full construction documentation, so the partner can build more units from the same design.
