Project Overview

Barrio San Cristobal is a rural community of approximately 300 residents in the Bolivian highlands. Families have gone without reliable drinking water and sanitation for years, and supplies run short in the dry season. The community historically depended on an aging deep well, expensive private water trucks, and rainwater collection, all of which force families to strictly ration water during dry periods.

EWB Cornell's International Subteam, in partnership with EWB-USA and FIEA Bolivia, is designing and building the water and sanitation system that will give the community water year-round.

The Challenge

  • Outdated deep well infrastructure unable to serve the full community reliably
  • Families forced to ration limited water supplies during the dry season
  • Reliance on expensive private water trucks places financial burden on residents
  • Inadequate sanitation creates health and environmental risks
  • No wastewater management infrastructure in place

The Design

The project is designed in two phases. Phase 1 focuses on constructing a sanitary piping network and septic tanks to manage wastewater safely. That reduces contamination risk and lays the groundwork for the water system. Phase 2 will address water distribution infrastructure to ensure every household has reliable, clean drinking water access throughout the year.

The team works with community members and local engineers so the system is affordable to maintain and can be repaired locally.

Community Partnership

In Spring 2025, our team conducted a site assessment trip where we completed over 70 household consultations, surveying water usage, sanitation conditions, and community priorities. That data drives the engineering design, so the system reflects what residents asked for.

Engineering Approach

  • Sanitary sewer piping network connecting all households
  • Septic tank systems with safe effluent management
  • Community-maintainable design with local materials where possible
  • Water distribution network with storage to buffer seasonal variability
  • Collaboration with FIEA Bolivia for local engineering oversight
